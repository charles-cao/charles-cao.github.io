2026

  1. ICDM
    Isolation-based Spherical Ensemble Representations for Anomaly Detection
    Yang Cao, Sikun Yang, Hao Tian, Kai He, Lianyong Qi, Ming Liu, and Yujiu Yang
    In IEEE International Conference on Data Mining (ICDM), 2026
    Abs arXiv Bib

    ISER is an unsupervised anomaly detection method that extends isolation-based techniques by using hypersphere radii as proxies for local density: small radii indicate dense regions and large radii indicate sparse ones. It runs in linear time and constant space. We further propose a similarity-based scoring method that compares ensemble representations against a theoretical anomaly reference pattern, and adapt the scoring function of Isolation Forest to reduce axis-parallel bias and better detect local anomalies. Experiments on 22 real-world datasets show that ISER outperforms 11 baseline methods.

  2. WWW
    Towards Token-Level Text Anomaly Detection
    Yang Cao, Bicheng Yu, Sikun Yang, Ming Liu, and Yujiu Yang
    In Proceedings of the ACM Web Conference (WWW), 2026
    Abs arXiv Bib Code

    Existing text anomaly detection methods work only at the document level and cannot tell which parts of a text are anomalous. We introduce token-level anomaly detection, which localizes anomalies within text, define anomalies at both document and token levels, and propose a unified framework for both. We also collect and annotate three benchmark datasets with token-level labels, covering spam, reviews and grammar errors. Our framework outperforms six baselines.

  3. MLJ
    TAD-Bench: A Comprehensive Benchmark for Embedding-Based Text Anomaly Detection
    Yang Cao, Sikun Yang, Chen Li, Haolong Xiang, Lianyong Qi, Bo Liu, Rongsheng Li, and Ming Liu
    Machine Learning, 2026
    Abs arXiv Bib

    TAD-Bench is a benchmark for systematically evaluating embedding-based text anomaly detection. It combines datasets from multiple domains and pairs embeddings from large language models with a range of anomaly detection algorithms, analysing how the two interact. The results show where these approaches succeed or fall short and which tasks they suit, informing more robust, efficient and generalizable detection systems.

  1. EMNLP
    Text Anomaly Detection with Simplified Isolation Kernel
    Yang Cao, Sikun Yang, Yujiu Yang, Lianyong Qi, and Ming Liu
    In Findings of the Association for Computational Linguistics: EMNLP 2025, 2025
    Abs arXiv Bib HTML Code

    Two-step methods that pair large language model embeddings with anomaly detectors perform well on text anomaly detection, but the embeddings are high-dimensional and dense, which makes them costly in memory and computation. The Simplified Isolation Kernel (SIK) maps them into low-dimensional sparse representations while keeping the features that signal anomalies. SIK runs in linear time and substantially reduces space requirements. On seven datasets it outperforms eleven state-of-the-art anomaly detection algorithms while remaining efficient.

  2. MIR
    Anomaly Detection Based on Isolation Mechanisms: A Survey
    Yang Cao, Haolong Xiang, Hang Zhang, Ye Zhu, and Kai Ming Ting
    Machine Intelligence Research, 2025
    Abs DOI arXiv Bib

    This survey reviews isolation-based unsupervised anomaly detection, which finds anomalies by randomly partitioning data, since anomalies are few and different and are therefore isolated quickly. Such methods have low time and memory cost, scale well, are robust to noise and irrelevant features, and need little prior knowledge or parameter tuning. We cover the main methods, including their partitioning strategies, anomaly scoring functions and algorithms, as well as extensions to streaming data, time series, trajectories and images, and outline open challenges and future directions.
